You can change the ordering of the effects by dragging and dropping the effect names — either the name in the rack or the name next to the on/off switch.

## Enabling and bypassing

Each effect has two controls for its active state:

- **Switchboard toggle** (left sidebar): Toggles the effect on or off. When toggled off, the effect is both deactivated and hidden from the rack.
- **Bypass button** (power icon in the effect heading): Deactivates the effect's audio processing but keeps it visible in the rack. This is useful for quickly A/B comparing with and without an effect, or for keeping your effect chain layout while temporarily disabling an effect.

A bypassed effect appears greyed out in the rack. Click the bypass button again to reactivate it. The close button (✕) on an effect removes it from the rack entirely, same as toggling it off in the switchboard.

## Panel buttons

The buttons in the panel heading act on all effects at once:

- **Randomise** (shuffle icon): Randomly turns effects on or off and shuffles their order.
- **Bypass all** (power icon): Bypasses every active effect, or reactivates them all if any are already bypassed.
- **Remove all** (unload icon): Removes every effect from the rack.

Right-clicking an effect — either its heading or its switchboard entry — opens a menu for copying and pasting effects, and for resetting that effect's parameters to their defaults. You can copy a single effect and paste it onto the same effect elsewhere, or copy the entire FX rack — every effect, its order and its settings — and paste it as a whole. This works within a preset or between presets: copy from one, load another, and paste.

Right-clicking the empty space of the switchboard or the rack opens the same copy and paste options for the whole rack, plus an option to paste a single copied effect.

## Mix

Every effect has a _Mix_ knob in its heading area. Mix blends the original incoming audio (the _dry_ signal) with the effect's output (the _wet_ signal):

- At 0%, you hear only the dry signal — the effect is effectively inaudible.
- At 100%, you hear only the wet signal — the original is fully replaced by the effect's output.
- In between, the two are crossfaded.

This lets you dial in the strength of an effect without changing any of its other settings — useful for subtle reverb tails, parallel compression, gentle distortion, and so on.

## Distortion
Distortion adds harmonics to a sound — anything from gentle warmth to aggressive grit. Choose a character from the Type menu, then set how hard you push it with the Drive knob. That's the whole workflow; everything else is there to shape the result.

It runs with 4× oversampling and is anti-aliased to stay clean and musical. It also has built-in gain compensation to keep the level steady as you crank the drive up; for the Legacy types you have to opt in to this using the Auto Gain switch, for the other types it's always on.

- **Drive**: how hard the sound is pushed into the distortion, from nearly clean at the bottom to fully driven at the top. Its range is scaled so each step of the knob adds a similar amount.
- **Punish**: stacks extra saturation stages after the first, like an amp cascading its gain stages. Turn it up for a denser, more compressed drive that holds its character as notes decay.
- **Tilt EQ**: shifts the tone going into the distortion. Turn it up to saturate the highs for a brighter result, or down to push the lows for a warmer, fatter drive.
- **Gain**: trims the level of the distorted signal before the Mix knob blends it back with the dry sound.

The types are grouped into two families in the menu. _Bite_ types are musical saturation, from clean warmth to hard clipping:

- **Tape**: warm and smooth. Rounds off the loud peaks while quieter passages and tails stay clean.
- **Valve**: rich, even harmonics that stay musical however hard you push, instead of collapsing into a harsh square.
- **Overdrive**: pedal-style grit with a threshold — a note cleans up as it decays, and playing harder brings the bite in.
- **Hard Clip**: clean, aggressive brickwall clipping.
- **Octave**: adds a hard octave-up edge, then saturates — squares up on hot notes and follows their dynamics.

_Mangle_ types are more extreme, adding new tones rather than just thickening the sound:

- **Bitcrush**: lo-fi digital crunch, made by lowering the effective sample rate. Higher Drive means coarser and grittier.
- **Ring Mod**: multiplies the sound against a low oscillator for clangorous, metallic, bell-like tones. Drive raises the pitch and depth of the effect.
- **Sine Fold**: folds the wave through a sine curve for interesting, chaotic harmonics.
- **Warp**: an asymmetric shaper that bends the wave lopsidedly into a gritty, off-kilter drive.
- **Wavefolder**: folds the peaks back on themselves for bright, metallic harmonics.

_Legacy types are the original distortion algorithms, kept so older presets and projects sound unchanged. They load with their Auto Gain switch off to preserve their original level; turn it on to give them the same steady loudness as the other types._

## Convolution Reverb
The convolution unit adds a reverb-like effect to the sound by utilising an impulse response (IR) selected using a dedicated [browser](./browsers). Floe offers some realistic reverb room IRs, but also IRs that produce unusual sound effects.

The set of available IRs depend on what sample libraries you have installed and their features. Floe always has a built-in set of impulse responses available. But additionally, some sample libraries expand this collection, such as the community [Antique IRs](/packages/antique-irs) library.

## Limiter

The limiter is a brickwall limiter: it catches loud peaks and holds your output firmly below a maximum level, letting you push a sound louder without it clipping. It's usually placed last in the rack so it can catch everything above it.

- **Gain**: drives the signal harder into the limiter before it's limited. Turn this up to make the sound louder overall; the limiter will hold the peaks in check.
- **Ceiling**: the maximum output level. A small safety margin below 0dB is recommended rather than setting this to exactly 0dB.
- **Mix**: as with other effects, blends between the dry and limited signal.

Three meters show what the limiter is doing: _In_ (the incoming level), _GR_ (how much gain reduction is currently being applied), and _Out_ (the final level).

The limiter measures true peak rather than just the level of individual samples, which means it also catches peaks that occur in between samples — the kind that can appear once audio is played back or converted to a lossy format like MP3. This makes the Ceiling a more reliable guarantee of the loudest the signal will actually get. To do this, it looks a tiny fraction of a millisecond ahead in the audio so it can react before a peak arrives. This delay is small enough (0.5 ms) that Floe absorbs it internally rather than asking your DAW to compensate for it, so you won't notice it or need to do anything about it.

Thanks to that same short lookahead, the limiter is fast and transparent for moderate amounts of limiting. Push it hard — asking for a large amount of gain reduction — and there's less room for the gain change to ramp smoothly, so you may start to hear it colour or slightly distort the sound. If that happens, try backing off the Gain or raising the Ceiling a little.
